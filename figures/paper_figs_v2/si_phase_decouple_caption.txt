Shared phase drift of the illumination pattern (low-density DNA-PAINT movie of main-text Fig. 3, 1809/cover1/2, SIMCODE pattern estimation). SIMCODE models the phase of each of the 6 illumination patterns as a fixed phase phi_ij (angle i, phase step j) plus a phase drift eps_i(t) at time t, shared by the 3 phase steps of angle i (the DMD shifts the pattern in discrete pixel steps, so a drift of the optics moves all 3 steps together). (A) Per angle: the per-step phases fitted independently in 3000-frame blocks (left) decompose into the shared drift estimated by the pattern estimation (middle; black: eps_i(t) on 150-frame knots; grey circles: the block mean of the left panel minus phi_ij) and the fixed phases phi_ij (right). (B) Test of the shared-drift assumption in 150-frame blocks: standard deviation over the 3 steps of the per-step phase deviation from the block's common shift (solid) and the same statistic for pure estimation noise (dashed; from two independent halves of the emitters, split by position). The 3 steps deviate from a common shift by 2.2 / 1.9 deg rms (angle 1 / 2), as expected from the estimation noise alone (2.0 / 1.9 deg), against a drift range of -12 to 12 deg and -18 to 13 deg.

INTERPRETATION: the step-specific deviation d is 1.09 / 0.99 x its noise reference (150-frame blocks); persistent part 0.47 / 0.24 deg. So the 3 steps of an angle follow one shared drift within the estimation noise (shared-drift model supported). The residual to phi + eps (res) exceeds the per-step precision because it also contains the error of the estimated eps (3-block running mean, other weighting) and the block-to-block change of the spatial phase field over the emitter population; both are common to the 3 steps and cancel in d. The lag-1 autocorrelation of d is expected from emitters whose windows span adjacent blocks.
