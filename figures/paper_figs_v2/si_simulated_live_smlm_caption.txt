--- caption text ---

We performed reconstructions using the 4 different methods on simulated moving microtubules. The simulation contains
15 microtubules with Brownian motion and spring-like mechanics in a 50 x 50 px field of 108.33 nm pixels, simulated over 65 000 frames of which the
first 1000 are skipped, and is rendered as our density simulations: the in-situ PSF of the 1809/2 data set with emitter heights 0-400 nm (median 118 nm),
the sCMOS camera of that data set, and its photon and background levels (median 621 detected photons per 6-frame window, 1.94 photons/px/frame, mean
on-time 20 frames). The reconstruction was divided into 64 blocks of 1000 frames, with each block resulting in a single reconstructed frame. One of the
input frames, as well as the ground truth for a single reconstructed frame (frames 32 000-33 000: all positions of the labelling sites during the
block), is shown in panel A, together with the reconstructions of that block (all localizations of the block; SIMCODE: localizations with a detection
probability >= 0.63 and a posterior precision < 0.15 px, the precision cut of the other figures). A full reconstruction movie can be found in the supplemental media. To quantify the reconstruction accuracy, we computed the Jaccard
index of each method for each reconstructed frame against the ground truth (all trajectory points of the block). Panel B shows the mean Jaccard index over
the 64 blocks for each method at 3 distance thresholds. SIMFLUX estimated its pattern from its SMLM localizations, which at this emitter density gives few
converged fits; DECODE was retrained for this PSF with its published training settings. Because each block has ~0.9 million
ground-truth points, the Jaccard index mostly measures how much of the moving structure is covered, so it grows with the number of localizations; the
localization error against the ground truth and the FRC per block are given in Table Sx.
