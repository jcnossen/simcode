# SIMCODE bead calibration

Install the TorchPSF web dependencies with `npm ci` in its web directory. Run `node serve.mjs` here for a local WebGPU preview, or `node serve.mjs --build` for a static site. The server discovers a sibling torch-psf checkout or uses `TORCH_PSF_WEB`.

The public app supports planar bead movies and planar bead Z stacks. For a TIFF stack, enter a signed step in nm or use its spacing metadata. Set frames per step to the number of repeated exposures at each plane. For a generated stack, leaving the step blank centers the scan on the bead plane and uses a total span of 2 nᵢ λ / NA². At the default optics this is 957 nm over 40 planes. The stack starts with zero added random drift. Both the step and drift can be overridden.

Open **Example: randomly generated bead movie** below the file inputs to watch a 400 × 400 movie (40 frames by default, adjustable under **Example frames**) render from beads sampled uniformly at the selected density. The example selector follows the chosen acquisition. Set random drift as a peak-to-peak excursion in nm per axis and bead density in beads/µm² of camera area. Each slide movie frame uses the current optical and camera settings, one 200 nm illumination pattern per selected axis, and independent Gaussian random walks in X, Y and Z. Each trajectory is rescaled once per movie to the selected excursion. The slide movie has an 80 nm mean-focus reference. The drift trace is relative to the first frame. The top row shows the frame, pattern zoom and true point-emitter PSF at the current focus. The centered drift trace below shows XYZ displacement relative to frame one. Use the frame slider or scroll over the movie to browse exposures. Hover over a location to see an enlarged ROI with the phase-matched illumination blended in. The generated movie uses random Noll 5-11 aberrations and shows its simulated pattern. During estimation, the overlay becomes available from preliminary fitted bead intensities and is replaced with the final fitted pattern. Axial drift is evaluated with a moving vectorial PSF lookup table. Beyond the measured stack range, the fitted pupil is extrapolated. Choose **Estimate** beside the movie input to fit the completed TIFF. The estimator replaces the input form and plays the current camera frame as it is read and fitted. Back to inputs restores the file selection. The estimator displays the current point PSF, optical search score, focus, Z6 astigmatism and per-frame drift. With a calibration stack it also displays the stack fit likelihood during pupil estimation. Closing the view during generation cancels it.

Choose **View pupil and aberrations** during or after fitting for the normalized amplitude, phase in waves and signed coefficients by Noll index. Movie-only fits profile Noll modes 5 through 11 against registered bead shapes, spatial power and the asymmetric third-order correlation of individual exposures. The live TorchPSF column chart shows the current estimate and, for generated examples, the simulated truth. Focus and even-mode signs can remain ambiguous. A calibration stack fits the same modes with through-focus data. The PDF report also includes through-focus PSF sections and is available separately and inside the archive.

Valid acquisition settings are remembered in a first-party browser cookie for one year. The optional stack step can be cleared to restore automatic TIFF spacing.

Supply one grayscale multipage TIFF movie (TYX). Set the bead diameter beside the movie input. Choose the number of axes, phases per axis (1–10), and whether the acquisition interleaves them. The angle-index preview shows one repeating cycle starting at axis 0 and phase 0°. Nominal steps are equally spaced over 360°. With at least three steps, select **Estimate phases individually** to fit each repeating exposure slot independently for each axis. The first slot is the 0° reference. Phase estimates appear in the result table and in each pattern's `phase_steps_rad` in modulation.json. One- and two-phase acquisitions support PSF, drift, and bead photometry. The single-channel planar Z stack is a standalone input. For a reverse sweep enter a negative step. The step override takes priority over the OME/ImageJ metadata. Confirm the example optical and camera settings for your acquisition.

TorchPSF renders a finite-bead vectorial focus table on WebGPU, seeds positions from the mean and its residual, and jointly apportions shared pixel counts to overlapping beads. Per-frame photometry and XYZ drift alternate with a common pupil fixed across the movie. XY drift is limited to ±4 pixels around the mean coordinates. Movie-only optics start with a signed coordinate search over Noll modes 5 through 11. With at least five complete illumination cycles and a field at least 96 pixels across, TorchPSF refines the shared pupil using through-focus diversity. Averaging a complete cycle reveals the same stationary bead field at different focuses. The optimizer fits relative complex optical transfer across cycles, eliminating the unknown bead texture and allowing lateral drift. It tries both focus signs. The fitter tracks individual exposures by comparing their spatial power spectra with repeats of the same illumination phase. It accounts for focus variation within each averaged cycle when refining the pupil. A weak fit or insufficient focus diversity leaves the coarser estimate in place. Review the fit summary to see which method was used. A stack uses the TorchPSF bead calibration on selected reference ROIs and fixes the multi-mode pupil before movie fitting. Localizations below optical resolution can remain merged.

SIMCODE fits the first temporal harmonic to each bead for each illumination direction, estimates the spatial carrier, corrects the phase for XY motion, and fits quadratic field surfaces. For dense random, stationary bead fields, field depth uses the ratio of first-harmonic image power to DC image power over complete cycles. A difference-of-Gaussians filter isolates spatial bead texture, and the diagonal phase covariances are corrected for photon shot noise. This assumes the optical blur varies slowly within a phase cycle and the local bead positions are spatially uncorrelated. The bead-trace estimate is retained as `bead_depth` in modulation.json. Sparse fields, small images and carriers too close to the texture band use bead photometry. Both depth estimates include the uniform fluorescent sphere correction. Angles refer to the wavevector measured from +x toward +y in image coordinates. Maps extrapolate in regions with few beads. Downloads include the reusable point-emitter PSF archive, frame-aligned bead fit, modulation coefficients and sampled fields, and the field overview PNG.

For generated movies, the preview renders the aberrated point PSF used for the simulation. The estimator displays the simulated and fitted PSFs together, with true and estimated Noll coefficients, focus, XYZ drift, pattern pitch, orientation, phase steps, wavevector components, modulation depth, phase and intensity ratio across the field. Drag across a depth or intensity-ratio map to inspect fitted values and available simulation truth at a camera location. Expand the field coefficients to inspect all terms of the quadratic surfaces. True and estimated depth and ratio maps share a scale in the overview PNG. The complete simulated pattern is available in true-modulation.json. Truth is retained in the browser for the selected generated movie and is used only for display. The Reconstruction panel overlays fixed bead positions on the selected frame after XY drift correction. For generated movies, blue crosses mark first-frame ground truth registered to the same static coordinate system. Zoom or pan to move the dotted ROI in the input movie. Zernike chart and table values are in milliwaves RMS (1000 mλ per wave). Uploading a TIFF runs the ordinary fit.

See [the matched depth benchmark](depth-benchmark.md) for true-pupil and true-drift controls on identical pixels.

On three sinusoidal-drift benchmark movies of 100 frames each of 12,000 planar 40 nm beads (two independent seeds, axial excursions of ±200 or ±500 nm), signed coefficient RMS error across Noll 5–11 was 0.0205, 0.0781 and 0.0086 waves for the coarse fit. The Python reference obtained 0.00029, 0.00058 and 0.00076 waves. WebGPU obtained 0.00025, 0.00079 and 0.00075 waves on the same pixels. The generator and fitter share a vectorial optics model. Experimental accuracy has not been measured in this comparison.

See the [seven-movie random-walk benchmark](https://github.com/jcnossen/torch-psf/tree/a820ba9/study/simcode_random_walk) for the Python simulator, focus refinement, and per-seed comparisons at 12,000 and 1,200 beads.


### Cross-validation for a 2D slide movie

Choose **Cross-validate PSF and modulation** after fitting a movie with at least four complete cycles. Two interior cycles are withheld in separate refits. The reported Poisson deviance per camera pixel measures prediction on those withheld exposures. A smaller value is better. No simulated truth or full-movie estimates enter the refits. Between the retained frames on either side of a gap, motion and background are linearly interpolated.

For the pupil comparison, the same phase-specific training bead brightnesses are rendered with the fitted pupil, an experimental two-round raw-pixel refinement, and a flat pupil. For the modulation comparison the fitted pupil is fixed. The training pattern (including the field depth, axis intensity ratios, phases and bead-size attenuation) predicts exposure brightness using one fitted brightness scale per bead; the baseline uses the bead's constant training brightness. Fewer than three phases skip the pattern comparison. Drift prediction, bead detection and overlap affect these predictive scores. The cross-validation JSON includes the fold coefficients and fit diagnostics.


On a seeded 400 × 400 browser example (seed 19472, 40 frames, six exposures per cycle, 150 mλ pupil RMS, 400 nm peak-to-peak drift per axis, 6.39 beads/µm²), doubling the phase-diversity iteration limit from 26 to 52 left the signed Z5–Z11 coefficient RMSE at 62.47 mλ. With the simulated focus supplied to the diversity solver for diagnosis, the error fell to 15.91 mλ. An independent saved example (first 40 frames of dense seed 17) gave 1.43 mλ at both limits. Truth was excluded from the following browser-example cross-validation scores.

| Withheld cycle | Fitted pupil | Raw-pixel refinement | Flat pupil | Fitted modulation | Constant brightness |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | 21.201 | 22.486 | 34.872 | 21.870 | 22.213 |
| 4 | 22.893 | 23.439 | 38.179 | 25.951 | 21.269 |

Each fold trained on 34 exposures and predicted six. The raw-pixel trial retained the fitted bead coordinates and per-bead fluxes and adjusted shared pupil and local XYZ, flux scale and background on up to eight training patches. It worsened predictions in both folds. The default exported pupil is therefore still the production fit. The pattern improved the first withheld cycle slightly and degraded the second.
